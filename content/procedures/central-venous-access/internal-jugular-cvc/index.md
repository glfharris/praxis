## Overview and scope

This guide covers ultrasound-guided Seldinger insertion of a short-term, non-tunnelled central venous catheter (CVC) into the internal jugular vein in adults. It assumes an operator trained in the technique, with appropriate supervision and an assistant. Dialysis and long-term devices require a separate access plan.[^aoa2025]

## Indications

Confirm that a CVC is needed and that the device, site and number of lumens suit the intended treatment. Common indications include:

- Delivery of irritant or vesicant medications
- Parenteral nutrition
- Difficult peripheral access when a suitable alternative is unavailable
- Chemotherapy, with device choice guided by the treatment plan
- Advanced haemodynamic monitoring, including central venous pressure measurement

Choose access for the anticipated treatment and duration, including whether a different device would better preserve future access.[^aoa2025]

## Contraindications

Assess the clinical need alongside the patient's wishes and the suitability of the proposed site. Obtain consent from a patient who has capacity; if capacity is lacking, follow the applicable consent and decision-making framework.

Reasons to reconsider the site or seek experienced help include overlying infection, thrombosis in the target vein, distorted anatomy, increased bleeding risk, or inability to tolerate the position or remain still. Assess bleeding risk in context rather than treating an abnormal laboratory result alone as a reason to abandon access.[^aoa2025]

## Equipment

{% <checklist title="Equipment"> %}
- CVC kit with introducer needle, guidewire, dilator, scalpel and suture
- Ultrasound machine with high-frequency linear probe, sterile probe cover and sterile gel
- Full aseptic barrier: hat, mask, sterile gown, gloves, large drape
- Skin prep — 2% chlorhexidine with 70% alcohol
- 1% lidocaine in a 10 mL syringe with a small hypodermic needle
- Saline flushes for each lumen with sterile injection ports
- Pressure transducer and tubing for confirming a venous waveform before use
- Sterile dressing and sharps disposal container
- An assistant
{% </checklist> %}

## Preparation

Explain the procedure and expected sensations to the patient, including local anaesthetic infiltration and the need to remain still. Establish a way for an awake patient to communicate discomfort beneath the drapes.

{% <checklist title="Before starting"> %}
- Confirm patient identity, indication, consent or decision-making arrangements, and intended site and side.
- Check allergies, including local anaesthetic, chlorhexidine and relevant device materials.
- Assess bleeding risk and anticoagulant treatment; review relevant results when indicated.
- Confirm an appropriate catheter, all equipment, monitoring and an escalation plan are available.
- Identify the operator, supervisor where required, and assistant; agree their roles and the verbal check for guidewire removal.
- Complete the team time-out using the local CVC checklist.
{% </checklist> %}

Use the local invasive-procedure safety process for sign-in, time-out and sign-out.[^aoa2025]

Perform a scouting ultrasound immediately before insertion to assess patency, vessel size and depth, and the relationship between the internal jugular vein, carotid artery and other structures.[^aoa2025] Use sterile single-use gel for this assessment as well as during insertion, and remove residual gel before skin antisepsis.[^ukhsa-gel]

Protect the bedding with an absorbent pad. Arrange the drapes to keep the patient's face and airway accessible. Maintain the security of any airway device; if its ties obstruct the field, agree a safe alternative with the clinician responsible for the airway before adjusting them.

Ensure appropriate monitoring, typically ECG, blood pressure and pulse oximetry. The assistant should help with positioning, reassure the patient and obtain additional equipment without breaking the sterile field.

## Positioning

Use a head-down tilt (Trendelenburg position) when clinically appropriate and tolerated, to improve venous filling and reduce the risk of air entrainment. Adapt positioning to the patient's respiratory and other clinical needs.[^aoa2016]

Turn their head slightly away from the side of insertion, opening up the available space. Too much rotation can lead to an overlap between the jugular and carotid, increasing the risk of accidental arterial puncture.

## Procedure

{% <step number={1} title="Prepare equipment"> %}
Open your equipment in a sterile fashion. Flush each lumen of the catheter and then close off the line so the flush is retained within it. Finally, arrange your equipment so that each item is easy to reach in the order you'll need it.
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={2} title="Prepare sterile field"> %}
If hair prevents adequate skin preparation or dressing adhesion, clip only the area needed before preparing the sterile field. Do not shave with a razor.[^nice-hair]

Move obstructing dressings or ECG electrodes as needed, maintaining monitoring and airway securement.

Perform hand hygiene and use a cap, mask, sterile gown and sterile gloves. Prepare the skin with 2% chlorhexidine in 70% alcohol, or an appropriate alternative if contraindicated. Allow it to dry fully according to the product instructions before puncture. Apply a full-body sterile drape and use a sterile probe cover with sterile gel.[^cdc-infection][^ukhsa-gel]

Orient the drape's transparent window so the patient's face can be observed, and keep the airway accessible.
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={3} title="Scan and identify vessels"> %}
Scan the neck to identify the vessels, paying particular attention to distinguishing the internal jugular vein from the carotid artery — use Doppler if there is any uncertainty. Also look for overlying superficial vessels, such as the external jugular vein, before choosing your approach.

{% <callout kind="tip" title="Identifying the vessels"> %}
The artery is often thicker walled and less compressible than the vein, but position, pulsatility and compressibility are clues rather than proof of vessel identity. Resolve uncertainty before puncture.[^aoa2016]
{% </callout> %}
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={4} title="Inject local anaesthetic"> %}
Check the local anaesthetic preparation, allergy history and maximum permitted dose, accounting for any local anaesthetic already given. Infiltrate 1% lidocaine at the intended puncture site and along the planned tissue tract, using incremental volumes. Avoid distorting the anatomy with excessive volume. Allow time for it to work and check its effect before proceeding.

Assess analgesia separately from sedation: a patient appearing deeply sedated does not, by itself, establish that local anaesthetic is unnecessary. Plan additional analgesia or sedation according to the patient's needs and the available monitoring and expertise.[^rcoa-sedation]
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={5} title="Access vessel"> %}
Attach the syringe to the needle or cannula and insert through the skin. Holding the probe with your non-dominant hand, advance under real-time ultrasound while keeping the needle tip identified. If the tip is lost, stop advancing and relocate it. Confirm entry of the tip into the vein with blood aspiration; blood return alone does not establish safe venous placement.[^aoa2025][^aoa2016]

{% <callout kind="tip" title="Needle or cannula"> %}
A cannula can be more fiddly to use than a needle, but may help reduce the risk of passing through the back wall of the vein or of the needle tip slipping out while you're inserting the guidewire.
{% </callout> %}
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={6} title="Insert guidewire"> %}
Insert the guidewire through the needle or cannula, keeping hold of it at all times.

There are markings on the guidewire to know how far you have inserted it. A distance of 15–18 cm is standard.

Ensure you are not forcing the wire at any point, as this may indicate that it has kinked or is abutting a vessel wall.

Have your assistant keep an eye on the cardiac monitor during guidewire insertion to assess for ectopic beats, which may indicate the wire has advanced too far into the right heart.
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={7} title="Confirm guidewire position"> %}
Use ultrasound in transverse and longitudinal views to confirm that the guidewire lies within the internal jugular vein. Follow its course as far centrally as the available view allows, checking that it has not passed through the vein into an adjacent structure. Remove the needle or cannula while retaining control of the wire.[^aoa2025]

Follow local guidelines on confirming guidewire position, as well as ultrasound confirmation, blood gases and pressure transduction are required in some centres.

{% <callout kind="danger" title="Confirm before dilation"> %}
Do not dilate unless venous placement of the guidewire is confirmed. If the findings are uncertain, stop and obtain experienced help or additional confirmation. Free wire movement or blood return alone is insufficient.[^aoa2025]
{% </callout> %}
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={8} title="Dilate skin and vessel"> %}
Decide whether to make a small skin incision or to dilate without cutting.

Pass the dilator over the wire, retaining control of the exposed wire throughout. Follow the original needle tract and advance only far enough to dilate the skin and tissue tract into the vein; do not insert the full dilator length routinely. Stop if the wire or dilator meets resistance, reassess the position and seek help rather than applying more force.[^aoa2016]

Remove the dilator while holding the guidewire directly and keeping it stable. Cover the puncture with sterile gauze and apply gentle pressure as needed to control bleeding.
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={9} title="Insert catheter and remove guidewire"> %}
Thread the catheter onto the wire. Before advancing the catheter into the patient, ensure the wire has emerged from the hub of the distal lumen and take hold of it. Advance the catheter to the intended insertion depth while maintaining control of the wire.

{% <callout kind="tip" title="Threading the catheter"> %}
Forming a loop in the guidewire and holding it near the end reduces movement of the tip, which makes threading easier. Remember that the wire will emerge from the distal port, so make sure this is unblocked beforehand to let the wire feed through.
{% </callout> %}

Hold the catheter in position and remove the guidewire completely. Inspect the wire to confirm it is intact. State **"wire out"** and obtain verbal acknowledgement from the assistant before proceeding. Include this confirmation in sign-out and documentation.[^ficm-cvc]
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={10} title="Secure catheter"> %}
Attach the catheter clamp and place four sutures to secure the catheter to the skin.
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={11} title="Aspirate and flush lumens"> %}
Fit bungs or injection ports onto each lumen to protect the line, then aspirate and flush each one. Use a 10 mL syringe for flushing, as smaller syringes generate higher pressures that risk damaging the vessel wall.
{% </step> %}

{% <step number={12} title="Apply dressing"> %}
Apply a dressing over the site, choosing between a chlorhexidine-impregnated dressing or a standard dressing according to local policy.
{% </step> %}

## Immediate checks

Complete these checks before authorising use, and record the findings. Venous placement, tip position and insertion complications are separate questions. Obtain and review a chest X-ray (CXR) before routine use of an internal jugular CVC.

### Is the catheter in the venous circulation?

Confirm guidewire removal and that blood can be aspirated and saline flushed through each lumen without resistance. Transduce the CVC and confirm an appropriate venous waveform and pressure before use.[^ficm-cvc]

If arterial placement remains a concern, stop and seek experienced assessment; paired catheter and arterial blood gases may help resolve uncertainty. A blood sample labelled "VBG" does not by itself establish the catheter's location.[^ficm-cvc]

### Is the tip appropriately positioned?

Review the CXR for catheter course and tip position, and document whether the position is acceptable for the device and intended treatment. An appropriate venous waveform establishes venous placement but does not establish the exact tip position. Use the device requirements and local policy to interpret the upper-body tip target.[^aoa2025]

### Is there an insertion complication?

Assess for bleeding, neck swelling, respiratory symptoms, arrhythmia and haemodynamic deterioration. A post-insertion CXR can assess catheter course and tip position and look for pneumothorax or haemothorax; it does not replace assessment of an acutely deteriorating patient.[^aoa2016]

{% <callout kind="warning" title="Urgent use before CXR"> %}
If delaying treatment to obtain a CXR would create greater risk, an experienced clinician must weigh and document the decision to use the line urgently. Confirm venous placement, including an appropriate transduced waveform and pressure, and assess for immediate complications. Document the checks, rationale and responsible clinician, and arrange a prompt CXR and review. This exception does not permit use of a line whose venous placement is uncertain.[^aoa2025][^ficm-cvc]
{% </callout> %}

## Aftercare and documentation

At sign-out, record the indication, site and side, catheter type and insertion depth, operator and supervision, number of attempts, guidewire removal, confirmation findings and any complications. State whether the line is cleared for use and identify who will review any outstanding imaging or other results. Hand over these details to the team responsible for the line.[^aoa2025]

Inspect the insertion site and dressing regularly, maintain aseptic handling of the hubs, and replace a dressing if it is damp, loose or visibly soiled. Review the need for the catheter each day and remove it promptly when no longer required.[^cdc-infection]

## Troubleshooting and complications

### Resistance or failed access

Stop advancing the needle, wire, dilator or catheter if its position is uncertain or unexpected resistance occurs. Reassess with ultrasound and obtain experienced help. Avoid repeated attempts without a new assessment and plan.[^aoa2025]

### Arterial puncture or catheter placement

For an uncomplicated needle-only arterial puncture, remove the needle and apply direct pressure, then reassess for bleeding or swelling. Expanding neck swelling or airway compromise needs urgent help.[^aoa2016]

{% <callout kind="danger" title="Arterial dilation or large-bore catheter placement"> %}
If a dilator or catheter of 6 Fr or larger has entered an artery, leave it in place, secure it and obtain urgent vascular surgical or interventional radiology advice. Do not treat this as a simple needle puncture. Do not use a catheter suspected to be arterial.[^aoa2025]
{% </callout> %}

### Missing or damaged guidewire

If the complete wire cannot be accounted for, stop and escalate urgently for imaging and specialist assessment. Do not attempt blind retrieval.[^aoa2016]

### Acute respiratory or circulatory deterioration

Stop the procedure, call for senior help and assess and support the airway, breathing and circulation. Consider pneumothorax, haemothorax, bleeding, air embolism or vascular/cardiac injury. Prevent further air entry by closing any open catheter ports. Do not delay emergency treatment while waiting for routine imaging.[^aoa2016]

### Arrhythmia

Stop guidewire advancement and withdraw it sufficiently to relieve suspected cardiac irritation while maintaining control. Assess persistent arrhythmia or haemodynamic instability urgently.[^aoa2016]

### Later infection or thrombosis

New site inflammation, discharge, fever, limb or neck swelling, or catheter dysfunction needs clinical assessment. Follow the local infection or thrombosis pathway; do not force a flush through a resistant lumen.[^aoa2025][^cdc-infection]
