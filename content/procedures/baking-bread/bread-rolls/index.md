## Overview

This non-clinical demonstration uses the dough from [the simple white loaf](@/procedures/baking-bread/simple-white-loaf/index.md). Follow that recipe through its first rise, then use the method below instead of shaping a tin loaf.

## Equipment

{% <checklist title="For eight rolls"> %}
- One batch of risen white-bread dough
- Kitchen scales and a dough scraper
- A baking tray lined with baking parchment
- A clean cover and a cooling rack
{% </checklist> %}

## Divide the dough

Tip the dough onto a lightly floured worktop. Divide it into eight equal pieces, roughly 105 g each. Weighing the pieces helps the rolls bake at the same rate.

Keep the waiting pieces covered while you work so their surfaces do not dry out.

## Shape

### Form a round

Fold the edges of each piece into its centre. Turn it over so the gathered edges sit underneath.

### Tighten the surface

Cup your hand loosely over the dough and move it in small circles against the worktop. Use gentle pressure to produce a smooth, rounded top.

{% <callout kind="tip" title="Use less bench flour"> %}
A little friction helps tighten the dough. If each piece slides around without changing shape, brush excess flour off the worktop.
{% </callout> %}

## Final prove

Arrange the rolls on the tray with space between them. Cover and leave for about 30–45 minutes, until puffy. Preheat the oven to 220°C conventional / 200°C fan.

{% <details title="Separate rolls or a batch?"> %}
Leave wider gaps for crust all around each roll. Place them closer together for a batch that joins during baking and pulls apart into softer sides.
{% </details> %}

## Bake and cool

Bake for 15–20 minutes, until evenly golden. Transfer to a rack with oven gloves and allow to cool before splitting.

## Check the result

The rolls should feel light for their size, with a set crust and a soft crumb. If their sizes or colours vary substantially, check portion weights and tray placement on the next batch.
